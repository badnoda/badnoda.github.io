The Tsunoda Lab (Field Swarm Robotics, University of Hyogo) aims to build **swarm robot systems that never get stuck, even in harsh environments**, combining control theory, bio-inspiration, and hands-on hardware development.

## 1. Shepherding-inspired guidance control of swarms

Inspired by how a sheepdog herds a flock of sheep, we study control laws in which a small number of guiding agents steer a large group of agents ("controlling 100 with 1").

* Theoretical analysis and stability of shepherding control laws (e.g., a globally stable shepherding controller based on backstepping)
* Identification of guidance models from biologging data of real sheepdogs and sheep (GMDH approach)
* Hardware experiments on scalable sheepdog-type swarm robot navigation using farthest-agent targeting control
* Guiding animal groups with rough-terrain mobile robots ("Robotic-Shepherding System")
* Related projects: JSPS KAKENHI Grant-in-Aid for Early-Career Scientists (2022–2024); Grant-in-Aid for Transformative Research Areas (A), publicly offered research (2024–2025); JST Economic Security Program on guidance and collision avoidance of small UAV swarms (2025–2029)

## 2. Field swarm robotics for traversing unknown environments

We develop navigation methods that let swarms of simple robots traverse and explore rough, unspecified environments without prior information.

* Swarm robot navigation exploiting interaction with the environment, e.g., sound fields and sound-wave diffraction
* Primitive navigation algorithms for swarm robots (*Robotics and Autonomous Systems*, 2026)
* Weakly constrained multi-robot systems; implicit control of centipede-type and multi-legged robots
* Related projects: JSPS KAKENHI Grant-in-Aid for Early-Career Scientists "Swarm robot navigation for adaptively traversing unspecified environments" (2025–2027); Kawanishi Memorial ShinMaywa Education Foundation research grant on cooperative object transport by swarm robots (2026–2028)

## 3. Robots for landslides and river-channel blockages

Based on the concept of "open design", we develop, together with collaborators, robot groups that make the initial response at landslide sites remote and automated.

* GREEMA, a robot that grows by taking in environmental material; i-CentiPot-Aqua, a multi-legged robot that takes in water to form its body; amphibious centipede-type robots
* Root-Anchor, a plant-root-inspired anchor for soft ground; BRAINS, a separable/combinable robot group system
* The public demonstration of the prototypes was covered by NHK and several newspapers (Nov.–Dec. 2025)

## 4. Data-driven control and adaptive control of multi-agent systems

With collaborators, we also work on data-driven PID controller design, adaptive control of multi-agent systems, and control system design involving humans, such as heart-rate control.
